## 2026-10-03: the status review's gaps and bugs, fixed

The status review of 2026-10-03 listed what the code could fix without outside help. Children's play-tests, coach
keying and a real phone still wait (Next 1, 2 and 9). What changed:

- **Playback speed.** Auto players now run to their formation targets at no more than 7 m/s (`timeline.js
  runTargets`), so a long pass no longer flings them across the pitch. The learner's base is still judged on the targets
  themselves, so no answer moved. Over the 36 drills (20 Hz, auto players), the 99th percentile speed fell from 14.1 to
  9.5 m/s and the fastest from 40.3 to 15.8 m/s; in Live (5 runs of 45 s) the 99th percentile fell from 16.5 to 12.3 m/s.
- **Offside at an imminent pass.** The timeline tags each frame with the next event within 1 s (`tags.nextEvent`), and
  the offside rule treats a teammate's pass that close as played. In m2-12 a spot 2.5 m offside at the back post now
  scores 59 (it scored 82).
- **Ten new rules**, so every v1 positioning principle but the structural F1-F3 has one (the passing ideas, PA, are
  passing.js's): recovery (T3, R4), half-space (P1),
  flank-share (B6), cross-defence (U8), drop-narrow and a delay mode for the press (T2), line-height (U3),
  concentration (U7), unity (B12), block-height (U6) and gk-angle-depth (G1). The role principles R1, R2, R4 and R5 are
  judged by the rules of their parts. Two new scene steps place what layer A could not: a winger inside when the
  full-back holds the wing, the ball-side #8 in the half-space (P1), and full-backs pushed up when the #6 drops between
  split centre-backs (B9, B5).
- **Engine answers instead of authored ones.** m1-06, m2-08, m2-10, m2-11 and m3-05 are now answered by the engine,
  0.8-3.5 m from the coach's spot. Only m2-05 keeps an authored answer (its `notes` say why).
- **The lesson cap.** A drill's own lesson rule (its first principle, weight 2 or more) scoring below 0.5 caps the spot
  at 69, so missing the lesson can no longer grade A or S. m2-09's start in front of their midfield drops from 94 to 69,
  and m2-07's spot in their #6's cover shadow from 90 to 69. No drill's best spot changed.
- **The goalkeeper.** Out of possession the auto keeper stands on the G1 spot (`formation.js keeperSpot`), and Explore
  offers the keeper as a role. Drills, Live and Player mode stay outfield.
- **Authored pass drills.** Two full-back "lead the runner" drills (`pa8-lb-01`, `pa8-lb-02`) fill the cell the pass
  generator never fills. `npm run index` and `npm run check` now handle pass drills, and the Road deals them to their
  position, mirrored to your side.
- **Offline play.** A service worker answers network first and falls back to the stored copy, so a phone that has
  opened the site once plays without a connection.
- **Refinements from a sweep of the new rules.** In a high block the front three are never asked past x 80
  (block-height), a loose ball is no cross and a centre-back covering the presser is not judged on crosses
  (cross-defence), a full-back comes just inside the wing lane when his winger holds the touchline 9-12 m ahead
  (scene.js, B6's other half), and two runners tied for the third ball-into-space target are both left out, so a frame
  and its mirror rate the same passes. Over 70 ball positions x 3 possessions x 10 roles, 94.8 % of best spots score S
  (94.4 % before the new rules).
- **A faster best-spot search where no heatmap is drawn.** The ten new rules made staging a rep slower (five reps took
  about 255 ms of CPU against 125 ms before, over the 300 ms test budget on GitHub's runner). `computeGhost` with
  `field: false` searches only the disc and skips cells whose zone alone caps them below the best found; it gives the
  same ghost on all 2,440 scenes checked and is about 6x faster. Staging, the generator, `npm run check` and Player mode
  use it: five reps now take about 110 ms.
- **Measured and left as is.** A full-back switch (PA6) cannot be generated or authored as one decision: every far-side
  receiver is 36-66 m away and the pass is rated too long; the real switch goes back through a centre-back first. The
  passing speed test "a set of 5 under 2 s" has failed now and then in a slow container (on the code before these
  changes too); `FOTBOL_PERF_SLACK=1.5 npm test` passes there.

## Known issues

### Drill and scenario content

- **The start picture can overlap tokens.** A drill judges on the free frame, where nobody reacts to where you stand (ARCHITECTURE §5.3). This keeps the score independent of the start spot and the drag. The cost is visual: your token at `learner.start`, or at your dropped spot in the replay, can sit on top of an opponent for a moment.
- **Authored answers.** Only m2-05 (B1, the full-back high and wide past their winger) uses `answer.mode: 'authored'`: the engine's best spot keeps the full-back 6.5 m behind the carrying centre-back, almost in their winger's cover shadow. Its `notes` explain why. m1-06, m2-08, m2-10, m2-11 and m3-05 moved to the engine's answer on 2026-10-03. `npm run check` prints the engine's spot for every authored scenario so this stays visible.
- **Fixed: a winger on the wrong side of their man.** The recovery rule (T3, R4) judges the side while we recover, critically past 1 m: in m1-06, 3 m on the wrong side scores 59 and 7 m scores 15 (they scored 81-85).
- **Lenient lessons, capped.** Missing a drill's own lesson now caps the spot at 69 (`LESSON_CAP`): m2-09's start in front of their midfield scores 69 (it scored 92-94) and m2-07's spot in their #6's cover shadow 69 (89-92). The cap needs the lesson's rule to weigh 2 or more and to score below 0.5, so a spot that half-meets the lesson can still grade B. m2-10 (B5) is now answered by the engine and its start scores 19.
- **Timing when authoring.** The automatic presser is decided from the lagged shape ball at key times and then blends in at 6 m/s or less, and every auto player runs to its target at 7 m/s or less (`runSpeed`), so a press scenario needs a ball key about 1.5 s before the freeze. The learner's base (`learnerBaseAt`) is judged on the targets themselves, but the auto players are drawn on their way.
- **Fixed: offside at a pass the ball keys play.** The timeline derives `tags.nextEvent` from the ball keys, and the offside rule treats a teammate's pass within 1 s (`passAhead`) as played, so m2-12's spot 2.5 m offside at the back post scores 59 (it scored 82). A pass more than 1 s after the freeze is still judged as open play.
- **`mirrorScenario` does not rewrite text.** Briefs, questions, takeaways and misconceptions must never name a side, and must never assume a gender. `tests/scenarios-content.test.js` enforces both.
- **m3-04 lists D8 (pressing triggers, v2) as a secondary principle.** Its Elo is tracked, but the principle page still says "coming later".

### Engine: formation (layer A)

- **One-phase data.** HELIOS has a single phase, so near our goal its shape is a box defence even when we have the ball. Near their goal it is an attacking shape even when we press. `phaseShape()` (`SHAPE_DEFAULTS`, mostly [D]) patches this:
  - Out of possession it levels and compacts the back line (U1 per player, with the line capped at x 52, U6). It brings a bypassed #9 back to the ball in a mid or low block (T3), brings the wingers in off the touchline, and tucks the far side in.
  - In possession it holds the width, spreads the back four and staggers the lines in build-up (B10).
  - scene.js then settles #8s and wingers goal-side of the opponents near them.

  This is a stand-in for authored tables (Next 2).
- **Layer A reads overlaps and the half-space through a scene step.** `scene.js shareFlanks` brings a winger inside when the full-back holds the wing lane, and a full-back inside when the winger holds the touchline just ahead (B6), and sends the ball-side #8 into the half-space when our full-back has the ball wide (P1); `salida` pushes the full-backs up when the #6 drops between split centre-backs (B9, B5). They fade continuously but are patches like `phaseShape()`; authored phase tables remain the fix (Next 6).
- **Build-up depth is a floor, not a shape.** In build-up the #6 and an #8 can stand on the same line from the ball, so the 30° angle check of support-distance fails. m2-02 needed an override.
- **High press.** With our line capped at x 52 (U6 [S]) and U1's 15 m gaps, the #8s hold at about x 67. The wingers stay within 15 m of the midfield, which makes a winger the #9's cover when their keeper has the ball on the byline (a 70 ghost at ball (95, 24)). Pressing triggers and cover shadows (D8, D9) are v2.
- **#6 depth in attack** is 20-24 m ahead of the back line with the ball in the final third. Rest defence (P13) is v2.
- **Full-back engagement (R2, U5).** With the ball wide in our half, the ball-side full-back ranks 10 m nearer the ball (`engageBias`). When the learner is that full-back, the #8 HELIOS puts near the ball stands on the press spot, and the ghost scores about 70 at ball (35, 14).
- **The GK.** Out of possession the auto keeper stands on the G1 spot: on the line that splits the ball's angle to the posts, 1 m off the line with the ball near goal and up to 16.5 m with it in their box (`keeperSpot`, [D]). In possession the keeper is fixed at (2.5, 34). Only Explore and the dev playground let you play the keeper.
- **Playback speed.** Auto players run to their targets at 7 m/s or less (`TIMELINE_DEFAULTS.runSpeed`). Over the 36 drills (20 Hz) 2.2 % of auto-player samples are faster than 8 m/s (it was 10.6 %) and the fastest is 15.8 m/s (40.3); in Live 1.2 % are faster than 12 m/s (5.5 %). What is left comes from the settle and separation steps, which can still snap when players cross, and two players crossing can overlap under 1 m for about 0.4 s.
- **Linear fallback:** the refitted i_y values differ slightly from RESEARCH. The documented [M] values are kept.

### Engine: scene, context, rules, score

- **The learner as the natural carrier (Explore).** The learner never carries. When the ball is dragged onto the learner's spot, a teammate takes it and the learner's base becomes that teammate's vacated spot (P6). There the learner is still judged by their own role's rules, so those ghosts can score 85-89.
- **Outside the canonical situations** (a sweep of 70 ball positions x 3 possessions x 10 roles, measured 2026-10-03), 94.8 % of ghosts score S (94.4 % on the same grid before that day's rules). The lowest are 55-77, all with a loose ball: two of our players nearly as near it as each other, or a full-back near halfway on the touchline. The new rules lost S in 11 cases and gained it in 19:
  - the ball-side centre-back on a cross from near the touchline (next bullet);
  - an #8 or the #6 who would be the natural carrier (the bullet above), now also judged by the half-space and support rules.
- **Crosses from near the touchline.** HELIOS puts the ball-side centre-back 5-9 m outside the near post when the ball is wide near our box, and the cross-defence rule (U8) wants him between the posts. Moving him in layer A broke his marking and the back line's compactness (tried on 2026-10-03: S fell from 95 % to 81-84 % there), so the rule stays and the ghost cannot fully meet it: over crossing positions (x 4-20 in the wing lanes, 7 defending roles, them on the ball or loose) 95.0 % of ghosts score S (97.1 % before the rule), and the rest 79-89. Authored phase tables would fix it (Next 6).
- **"20 m from the ghost scores ≤ 40" (RESEARCH 9.5)** holds only outside the ghost's 15 m search disc. A spot 20 m from the ghost on the base's side can score up to 50 for the #9 and #8s.
- **Borderline S:** final-third RCM scores 90. Rule tuning can drop it below 90 and fail the integration test, which is intended.
- **Filling the box (P10)** checks one runner at a time. `compact` is a learner-level check only: team length and width (U1) are not judged.
- **Separation** is continuous but only partial for two players crossing, and a squeezed player can end a few centimetres short of `minSeparation`.
- **Presses are re-decided only at timeline key times.** Put a ball key every 1-3 s in long carries.
- The speed tests use the wall clock. They take the median of several runs after a warm-up and have generous bounds, so a busy laptop passes them; a very slow machine or CI runner can scale the bounds with `FOTBOL_PERF_SLACK` (e.g. `FOTBOL_PERF_SLACK=3 npm test`).

### Engine: passing and generated drills

- **The passing numbers are teaching priors, not measurements.** A safe (green) pass never scores below 75 (`safeFloor`: 2 stars, unless it is too safe), a pass that is not safe pays a risk premium of 20 points (`riskWorth`; 20-40 star the same passes), a risky or too-safe pass is capped at 74 (1 star), a cut-out one at 45 and a critical one at 30, and a marker within 3 m of a runner tracks him (`markReach`). All [D] (research/passing.md has the reasoning); none has been checked against coaches' answers (Next 2).
- **The authored pass drills rest on the race model.** Both full-back drills star a ball into space because the runner wins the race (`pWin`) against a defender who stepped in front of them. Those numbers are [D] and unkeyed (Next 2), so a coach should confirm the two answers.
- **The yield tables are small samples.** `PASS_YIELD` and `SPOT_YIELD` are the share of calls that give a drill, measured on 8 seeds a cell (the zeros re-checked on 24 more); the set builders skip what they say cannot be made. Re-measure after changing a rule or a generator (tests/passdrill.test.js and tests/spotdrill.test.js spot-check the zeros). The zero cells are listed in Next 3 and 4.

### Player mode

- **Nobody aged 10-14 has used it yet** (Next 1), and it has not been tried on a real phone (the checks used Chrome's touch emulation) or heard on real speakers. The copy passes the reading-age and word-budget checks, but those are proxies.
- **Level pace and XP are defaults.** `LEVEL_XP` and the XP table ([D]: five 1-star plays reach level 2, a strong first session ends at level 3, First Team takes about eight strong sets) wait for play-testing with children. Try again no longer earns anything (it is practice), so it cannot speed levels up.
- **Fixed:** pass sets no longer repeat the receiver. road.js caps any one receiver at 2 best passes per set (`maxSameBest`); a winger's set therefore holds 2 forward bests instead of 3 (the sweep checks both).
- **Borrowed positions.** Where your position rarely gets an idea (Next 3 and 4), a rep is played in another position ("Now you're the left centre-back"): at most 2 a set (a third only when nothing else is left), but 19-29 % of a group's spot reps and 3-29 % of its pass reps (3 seeds of every node, measured 2026-10-03).
- **Set building time.** Measured in Node on a laptop over every node for every group (seed 1 and the set begun again): spot sets median 28 ms, 90th percentile 240 ms, slowest 0.5 s; pass sets median 160 ms, 90th percentile 670 ms, slowest 0.9 s (a defender's "Find the Free Side"). An old phone will be several times slower, behind "Getting the pitch ready" (Next 7). Asking the spot generator for words the set has not used (`avoidTemplates`) cut its calls by 15 % (599 to 510 over those builds) and spot-set time by 17 %; most of the drills still turned away break "one player on the ball per set" (about 250 of 270).
- **Generated drills can still read alike.** A set never asks the same question twice (7 templates), but across sets the questions repeat, and a stood-still miss always says "The ball moved, so you need to move too."
- **Crowded pictures.** Labels are placed to keep off YOU and its tag, then off the other figures and the ball ("Best spot" by the ring where it covers nobody, else a short callout with a line from the ring; a cue's label on whichever side covers least; a pass reveal labels at most 4 options, each placed by its own player's feet where it covers least, with that player's base ringed in its colour, and its camera eases onto the play: see Progressive field below), but a label can still touch YOU when there is no room, and at the full match on a phone figures 1-2 m apart still overlap (small games are drawn much bigger; figures that would hide a head or a number are drawn up to 1 m apart, which is not always enough there).
- **A "Who's open?" set left early is not put on the Road.** Its reps' records (Elo, history, rewards) are kept, but only a finished pass set records node stars; a "Find your spot" set left early goes on the Road as a shorter set.
- **Coach-only badges.** Kick-off (finish the tutorial) and Explorer (5 S spots in Explore) can be earned only on those Coach screens, and Coach mode earns nothing, so now they can be earned only by opening `#/learn` or `#/explore` while in Player mode, which no Player screen links to. Player mode's card hides them until earned; they want Player-mode conditions or retiring.
- **Match day's level.** Match day is Coach mode's Live underneath (the same sequences, scoring and grace), so it is as demanding as Live; whether a new player can earn stars there has not been tried with children. A gentler first run (a slower sequence, or the glow aid) is a candidate if it proves too hard.
- **Loading.** A cold first open loads about 70 modules. `python3 -m http.server` (npm run serve) sometimes drops one under that load; the router retries, but a dependency the browser has already failed to load needs the reload that the failure card offers. GitHub Pages does not drop them.

### Player mode's right area (forgiving stars, 2026-09-29)

- **The owner's raw targets at 5 m cannot all hold at once.** On the ring exactly 3/5/7 m from the best spot, raw, 3 stars at 3 m is 80.8 %, 2+ at 5 m 72.1 % and 1+ at 7 m 88.8 % (over the whole disc 88.4 / 83.0 / 95.4 %); on every spot that is not one of the targets' own exceptions it is 100 / 100 / 100 %. What is left is the relationships the targets say must stay wrong: at 5 m, pressing from level with or past the ball (6 %), the wrong side of your man (3.6 %), the start and standing still (7.2 %) and the lesson clearly failed (11.2 %). The dial `{ lessonCap: 2, pressAngleKey: -1, markAngleKey: -1 }` (ARCHITECTURE §5.18) gives raw 84.1 / 83.2 / 88.8 % at the price of a wrong-way press or a mark beside his man scoring Great; it is not applied.
- **"Close them down" forgives the angle.** In a D1 lesson only the side of the ball is a key (level with it or past it: 1 star); the angle is D2's (a D2 lesson keys pressing from the outside). So in D1 reps 954 of 3,648 spots 2-3 m off the best spot (26 %) earn 3 stars while the press rule scores under 0.1 there: goal-side of the carrier but off the line to goal, or on the wing pressing from the outside. A D1 angle key (the press rule at `pressRef` in the spot's direction, as D2's) would make the D1 green a wedge too; coach keying (Next 2) should decide whether a D1 rep teaches the angle.
- **The green in a crowd.** It is drawn under the figures (so they stay readable), so in a crowded press at a bigger game or the full match on a phone most of it can be hidden, and after a near miss the arrow into it (to 0.75 m inside the edge) is often only 2-3 m long, under YOUR figure. The ring, "Best spot" and the line still carry it. A press's green is a wedge (cut by the side of the ball and, in a D2 lesson, the wrong way round), which reads less like an area than a disc. A v2 could lift its edge over the figures or dim the figures at the reveal.
- **Elo and the rewards follow the kinder stars.** Elo learns `kidScore` (Coach mode's score held in the band of the stars shown), so sticker mastery (2 stars or more on recent plays) and levels come sooner than before; retune `LEVEL_XP` and the sticker threshold after the first play-test with children if they come too easily.
- **Match day's thresholds are a bot's.** Hot in the right area of the last second (`liveMemory` 1 s) and a run's stars from its time Hot (75 / 50 / 30 %) were set against a simple bot, not a player.
- **Keeping an attacker onside never happens near a best spot in the corpus** (nobody waits behind our line at the freeze), so that key is tested on a fixture only; offside at a pass likewise never comes within 5 m of a best spot.
- **Some fixes say "a little" when you are far away** ("Stay a little wider, so there is no big gap beside you." 15 m across; "Move a little closer to your number 2." at 7 m): the rules' own simple words, older than this change.
- **The drawn green is conservative:** it is traced along 48 rays from the best spot, so 2.2 % of 3-star spots (past a key's wedge, round a misconception circle) are left out of the drawing, and on a sliver between two rays 0.02 % of the drawn area is not 3 stars.

### Progressive field (small games, figures, the ball)

- **Defenders and #8s get few small passing games.** Measured over 6 seeds (every node's first set for each group, 2026-09-28): planned small slots were played small in 95 % of spot sets and 92 % of onboarding sets, but 73 % of pass sets (defenders 27 of 72, midfielders 52, strikers 60, wingers 70) and 71 % of quick "Who's open?" sets (defenders 1 of 12). A full-back's forward receivers bring in the offside line, which leaves no room in six players for the defenders in the lanes, so the smaller game would change the answer and cast.js plays it bigger. The compact pass casts (ARCHITECTURE §5.17) helped defenders most: generated pass drills small 34 → 42 % (full-backs 2 → 20 %, centre-backs 16 → 29 %), and on the Road (6 seeds x 4 groups, every pass node's first set and the quick set) small slots played small 294 → 333 of 408 (defenders' pass nodes 30 → 49 of 90, their quick sets 1 → 4 of 12), sets with no small game 16 → 7 of 144. The #8s' barely grew (44 → 45 % of their drills), and the #6's reached 36 %.
- **Fixed: the first Road set replayed the tutorial.** The onboarding set was never anyone's "last set", so the first node's set (the same easy drills in your position that can be played small) dealt 2 of its 3 drills straight back for a defender and a striker, the worked example among them, and the next node's recall rep brought the tutorial's drill back again. `road.js buildFirstSet` now records its reps as the last set (`startSet(app, 'first', reps)`, nothing counted), the next set takes the chapter's other ideas and more borrowed reps before them, and they stay in the last set through that set; the next two Road sets deal none of them (measured with the app's seeds for every group), and the sweep checks it.
- **The striker's onboarding set** plays its third rep as a bigger game (4 v 4): only two easy striker drills can be played small. The spot Road for every group plays 95 % of its small slots small.
- **The watch's camera covers the whole clip.** In "Find your spot" a small game's camera is steady through the watch and takes in everywhere the play goes, so a clip that starts with the ball far from where it freezes shows the start small and to one side. Fixed at the whistle: the board now eases in on the freeze (`play.js answerCamera`: before it, a rect for the freeze alone would have been 0.73 of the clip's area at the median, under 0.6 for 9 of the 56 staged authored reps), and back out for "See what happens". - **Briefs that cannot name their player.** The question, the line, Why? and the cue always say whom they mean when the picture shows more than one of a group (by shirt number, or they are not shown), but the brief shown while the play runs is kept as written when naming the player would take it past 12 words or it cannot say whom it means (`play.js repWords`): measured [M] 2026-09-29 over every authored drill and mirror at every stage it plays at, 15 of the 36 drills still say "their midfielder" or "your defender" with two or more drawn (58 of the 184 reps they play as, 28 of them smaller games; 11 drills because the number would pass 12 words, 4 because the words cannot say whom: m2-03, m2-10, m2-12, m3-10). Shorter briefs would fix it.

- **Camera edges.** Easing back to no camera (the full match after a small game) shows the letterbox strips until it lands, as the whole pitch always has. In headless Chrome the first frame or two after a board is made report stale layout, so the play page can resize the board on consecutive frames: the board fits its window again each time and the grass covers it, but the viewBox changes twice (the same zoom, a taller or shorter view); that is page layout (css/play.css, play.js), not the camera.
- **Wide small games on a phone held upright.** A smaller passing game whose lesson needs a player at each touchline (a switch, a full-back's options) still makes the camera wide, and its figures little bigger than the full match's. The compact casts made most small games tighter (their width across the pitch 29 → 21 m at the median): in the end-to-end re-check on a 375 x 812 phone the choice's figures grew from 40.6 to 51.6 px at the median (7.2 → 11.2 px/m), but 2 of 7 small games and 3 of 11 bigger ones were still 45 px or less.

### App and UI

- **Mode loading.** A cold browser loads about 60 modules. A busy local server (python `http.server`) occasionally drops one. The router retries a failed mode once, `fetchJSON` retries a data file once, and otherwise the failure card offers **Try again**.
- **Board orientation.** Every board mode (Learn's tutorial, Explore, Drill, Live, Author) pins its orientation from the window size (`session.js orientationFor`), so a phone held upright keeps one vertical pitch whatever the sheet does to the board's box. Only the dev playground offers `'auto'`.
- **Small screens.** Tokens are drawn at least 22 px across (`board.js tokenScale`, up to 1.8x life size) and pitch labels at least 11 px tall (`labelScale`; Player mode's at least 16 px and YOUR name tag's text at least 14 px). A drill names the pitch length its play needs (`board.setFocus`), and a phone held upright crops the rest of the length, so a drill draws about 1.4x bigger there. Live, Explore and the tutorial keep the whole pitch (their play spans it), so their tokens stop at the 1.8x cap (about 20 px on a 375 px phone) and players standing 1-2 m apart overlap. The board's height follows the collapsed sheet, which differs by phase, so on a phone the pitch shifts a little between phases.
- **Touch input.** Drags are relative (the token keeps its offset from the finger). The token eases up above the finger only after 24 px of travel, by at most 4 m, so a short corrective drag moves it exactly as far as the finger. A tap is judged by the drawn token (`board.js tapAction`): with a token armed, a tap on it disarms it and a tap anywhere else, however close, moves it there; in Player mode (tap-to-move) only a tap on YOUR drawn token picks YOU up, and a tap anywhere else, your name tag included, moves YOU there. None of this has been tried on a real phone yet (the checks used Chrome's touch emulation).
- **Drill sessions** can be carried on after leaving the page (same practice, same position, same browser tab, within 6 hours: `sessionStorage`). An unfinished session in another tab or after closing the tab is not kept, and only the latest unfinished session is.
- **The author preview's reveal** does not re-render if the wording setting changes mid-reveal.
- **Live-mode fps** has not been measured on a real phone. In a desktop browser it holds 60 fps, a scene analysis takes about 1 ms and a heatmap about 1 ms.
- **Resource data.** The free/paid flags of some resources are assumptions (Soccer Coach Weekly, The Outfield, U.S. Soccer grassroots). S6's `learnMore` uses the IFAB Law 11 URL with a label pointing to Law 14.
- **Rewards.** Coach mode earns nothing by design (adults share the player's store): its Drill, Explore, Live and tutorial reward hooks earn and show rewards only on a Coach route opened from Player mode, where Live awards only a run played to the end without the best spot on show and a rep's level-up shows with beat 2 (the header pill catches up then, or on the next route change). Coach mode still shows the player's level pill, card and trophy room. The sounds have not been listened to on real speakers yet (volumes are guesses). The Sunset orange kit is close in hue to the learner's orange highlight (the learner still has its glow, white edge and name tag).
- **Copy review.** The kid headlines and every rule text are our own paraphrases and need a content review. No module lists F8, B12, R4 or R5 among its principles (R4 and R5 appear only as secondary principles of a few scenarios), and Module 4 (Transitions) from RESEARCH 7.3 is not modelled.
